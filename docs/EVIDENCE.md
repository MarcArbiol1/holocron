# Evidence base for the gym-programming rules (researched 10 Sep 2026)

Method: every number below was pulled from the primary abstract or full text. Flags note anything not verifiable or weak.

## 1. WHO 2020 guidelines
Rule: Adults 18–64: 150–300 min/wk moderate OR 75–150 min/wk vigorous aerobic; muscle-strengthening >=2 d/wk. 65+: same, plus multicomponent balance + strength >=3 d/wk. 5–17: average 60 min/day MVPA; vigorous aerobic + muscle/bone-strengthening >=3 d/wk.
Source: Bull et al. 2020, Br J Sports Med 54:1451, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2020-102955.
Plain: Two and a half hours of brisk movement a week plus two strength days; older adults add three balance days.

## 2. ACSM 2009 progression models
Rule: Novice: 8–12RM, total-body, 2–3 d/wk. Intermediate: 1–12RM periodised, 3 d/wk total-body or 4 d/wk upper/lower "thereby training each major muscle group twice". Advanced: 4–6 d/wk, muscle-group split. Strength 1–6RM with 3–5 min rest; hypertrophy 6–12RM with 1–2 min rest; raise load 2–10% when 1–2 reps over target; endurance 40–60% 1RM, >15 reps, <90 s rest.
Source: ACSM 2009, Med Sci Sports Exerc 41:687, DOI 10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181915670.
Flag: the stand does not state "48–72 h"; it notes the anabolic environment stays elevated "up through 36–48 h". Use point 15 for recovery.
Plain: Beginners do whole-body workouts 2–3 times a week; splits come later.

## 3. Weekly sets per muscle (hypertrophy)
Rule: Schoenfeld 2017: each extra weekly set = +0.37% growth; <5 / 5–9 / 10+ split only a trend (P=0.074). Baz-Valle 2022 (trained men): 12–20 weekly sets; >20 not better except triceps. Pelland 2026 (67 studies, n=2058, fractional counting: indirect sets = 0.5): hypertrophy minimum effective dose 4 fractional sets/wk; 5–10 high efficiency; 11–18 intermediate; 19–29 lower; 30–42 lowest. Strength: MED 1 set/wk; 2 high efficiency; 3–4 intermediate; >=5 no consistent further gain. Per-session preprint: no further benefit beyond ~11 fractional sets/session (hypertrophy), ~2 direct sets/session (strength).
Sources: Schoenfeld, Ogborn, Krieger 2017 J Sports Sci 35:1073, DOI 10.1080/02640414.2016.1210197. Baz-Valle 2022 J Hum Kinet 81:199, DOI 10.2478/hukin-2022-0017. Pelland 2026 Sports Med 56:481, DOI 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w. Remmert preprint sportrxiv.org/index.php/server/preprint/view/537 (not peer-reviewed).
Plain: About 4 sets a week starts growth, 10–20 is the sweet spot, and each extra set buys less.

## 4. Frequency
Rule: Non-volume-equated: >=2x/wk per muscle beats 1x (ES 0.49 vs 0.30). Volume-equated: no difference (25 studies). Pelland 2026: frequency negligible for hypertrophy, but strength rises 1->2 sessions/wk (12.7%->17.3%).
Sources: Schoenfeld 2016 Sports Med 46:1689, DOI 10.1007/s40279-016-0543-8. Schoenfeld 2019 J Sports Sci 37:1286, DOI 10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906.
Plain: Hit each muscle at least twice a week; how you spread the sets matters less than doing them.

## 5. Load
Rule: Low load = <=60% 1RM, high = >60% 1RM, all sets to failure. Hypertrophy equal; 1RM strength favours heavy.
Source: Schoenfeld, Grgic, Ogborn, Krieger 2017 J Strength Cond Res 31:3508, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000002200.
Plain: Light or heavy both build muscle if you push hard; only heavy makes you strong at heavy lifts.

## 6. Proximity to failure
Rule: Refalo 2023: failure vs non-failure ES 0.19 (trivial). Robinson 2024: hypertrophy improves as sets end closer to failure; strength unaffected across a wide RIR range. Encode: hypertrophy 0–3 RIR; strength 2–5 RIR.
Sources: Refalo 2023 Sports Med 53:649, DOI 10.1007/s40279-022-01784-y. Robinson 2024 Sports Med 54:2209, DOI 10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2.
Plain: Get close to failure for size; you don't need to for strength.

## 7. Rest intervals
Rule: Trained men, 1 vs 3 min: 3 min gave greater 1RM squat/bench and thigh growth. Untrained 60–120 s sufficient; trained need >2 min for max strength.
Sources: Schoenfeld 2016 JSCR 30:1805, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000001272. Grgic 2018 Sports Med 48:137, DOI 10.1007/s40279-017-0788-x. Grgic 2017 Eur J Sport Sci 17:983, DOI 10.1080/17461391.2017.1340524.
Plain: Beginners 1–2 min; experienced lifters 2–3 min on big lifts.

## 8. Minimum effective dose
Rule: Strength (trained): 1 set x 6–12 reps at ~70–85% 1RM, 2–3x/wk, to failure. Time-poor: >=4 weekly sets per muscle, 6–15RM, minimum one leg press, one upper push, one upper pull, bilateral multi-joint; supersets/drop sets halve time; exercise-specific warm-up only.
Sources: Androulakis-Korakakis 2020 Sports Med 50:751, DOI 10.1007/s40279-019-01236-0. Iversen 2021 Sports Med 51:2079, DOI 10.1007/s40279-021-01490-1.
Plain: One hard set of a big lift, two or three times a week, still makes you stronger.

## 9. Mortality
Rule: Momma 2022: muscle-strengthening -> 10–17% lower all-cause, CVD, cancer, diabetes risk; J-curve, max at ~30–60 min/wk; combined with aerobic lower still. Shailendra 2022: max 27% reduction at ~60 min/wk. Gorzelitz 2022 (n=99,713): aerobic-only HR 0.68; aerobic + lifting 1–2x/wk HR 0.59.
Sources: Momma 2022 BJSM 56:755, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2021-105061. Shailendra 2022 Am J Prev Med 63:277, DOI 10.1016/j.amepre.2022.03.020. Gorzelitz 2022 BJSM 56:1277, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2021-105315.
Flag: no study directly tests full-body + cardio vs split in infrequent exercisers; inference: 1–2 days -> full-body (every muscle >=1–2x/wk) plus aerobic.
Plain: An hour of lifting a week plus some cardio cuts your risk of dying early; more lifting doesn't add.

## 10. Cardio
Rule: VO2max: endurance +4.9, HIIT +5.5 mL/kg/min. HIIT dose: even <=30 s intervals, <=5 min/session improve VO2max vs control. Weekend warrior: 1–2 sessions/wk meeting 150 min -> all-cause HR 0.92 vs regular 0.85 (NS difference). No minimum bout length in 2018 US guidelines.
Sources: Milanović 2015 Sports Med 45:1469, DOI 10.1007/s40279-015-0365-0. Wen 2019 J Sci Med Sport 22:941, DOI 10.1016/j.jsams.2019.01.013. Dos Santos 2022 JAMA Intern Med 182:840, DOI 10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.2488. Khurshid 2023 JAMA 330:247, DOI 10.1001/jama.2023.10875. Piercy 2018 JAMA 320:2020, DOI 10.1001/jama.2018.14854.
Plain: Total weekly minutes matter, not how many days you spread them over.

## 11. Warm-up
Rule: Warm-up improved performance in 79% of criteria. Static stretching: strength -5.4%, power -1.9%; >=60 s per muscle -4.6% vs <60 s -1.1%; dynamic stretching +1.3%. Verified ramp protocol: 20 min bike at 60% HRmax + 1x8 @50% + 1x3 @70% raised leg-press 1RM 8.4% vs specific-only.
Sources: Fradkin 2010 JSCR 24:140, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181c643a0. Simic 2013 Scand J Med Sci Sports 23:131, DOI 10.1111/j.1600-0838.2012.01444.x. Behm 2016 Appl Physiol Nutr Metab 41:1, DOI 10.1139/apnm-2015-0235. Abad 2011 JSCR 25:2242, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181e8611b.
Flag: "5–10 min general + 50/70/85%" is NSCA convention; the 85% step is not from a trial.
Plain: Get warm, move dynamically, then build up to your working weight in 2–3 light sets; skip long static holds.

## 12. Age
Rule (older adults): 1–3 sets x 8–12 (or 10–15) reps at 70–85% 1RM, 8–10 exercises, 2–3 non-consecutive d/wk; beginners/frail 1 set, lighter (20–30% start); avoid failure. Youth (NSCA): 1–3 sets x 6–15 reps, 2–3 non-consecutive d/wk, +5–10% load, light loads first, supervision; injury rate 0.012 per 100 participant-hours (RT) vs 0.80 rugby.
Sources: Fragala 2019 JSCR 33:2019, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000003230. Dent 2018 J Nutr Health Aging 22:1148, DOI 10.1007/s12603-018-1139-9. Faigenbaum 2009 JSCR 23:S60, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0b013e31819df407. Lloyd 2014 BJSM 48:498, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2013-092952; Stricker/AAP 2020 Pediatrics 145:e20201011 (abstracts only).
Plain: Older lifters train the same way with more care; teenagers lift safely if supervised and technique-first.

## 13. Sex
Rule: Same programme for both sexes. Hypertrophy ES 0.07 (NS); upper-body relative strength favours females. Menstrual phase: trivial early-follicular dip (ES -0.06, low-quality); "premature to conclude" any effect. Do not encode phase-based programming.
Sources: Roberts, Nuckols, Krieger 2020 JSCR 34:1448, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000003521. Hunter 2014 Acta Physiol 210:768; Hunter 2016 MSSE 48:2247. McNulty 2020 Sports Med 50:1813, DOI 10.1007/s40279-020-01319-3. Colenso-Semple 2023 Front Sports Act Living 5:1054542, DOI 10.3389/fspor.2023.1054542.
Plain: Men and women respond the same; the app should not change the plan by sex or cycle phase.

## 14. Body weight
Rule: 150–250 min/wk moderate prevents gain / modest loss; >250 min/wk for clinically significant loss. STRRIDE: aerobic and aerobic+resistance cut fat mass more than resistance alone; resistance needed for lean mass. Each 1 kg lost = ~4 kg less knee load per step. Protein plateau 1.62 g/kg/day.
Sources: Donnelly 2009 MSSE 41:459, DOI 10.1249/MSS.0b013e3181949333. Willis 2012 J Appl Physiol 113:1831, DOI 10.1152/japplphysiol.01370.2011. Messier 2005 Arthritis Rheum 52:2026, DOI 10.1002/art.21139. Morton 2018 BJSM 52:376, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608.
Flag: "low-impact first" is clinical convention supported by knee-load biomechanics.
Plain: Heavier users need more weekly minutes, cardio for fat, lifting for muscle, and ~1.6 g protein per kg.

## 15. Order and recovery
Rule: Large->small, multi-joint->single-joint (ACSM). Exercises placed first get more reps and greater strength gains. Muscle protein synthesis +112% at 3 h, +65% at 24 h, +34% at 48 h -> >=48 h before re-training a muscle.
Sources: Simão 2012 Sports Med 42:251, DOI 10.2165/11597240-000000000-00000. Phillips 1997 Am J Physiol 273:E99, DOI 10.1152/ajpendo.1997.273.1.E99. Damas 2016 J Physiol 594:5209, DOI 10.1113/JP272472.
Plain: Big lifts first, and give a muscle about two days before hitting it again.

## 16. Core
Rule: 7 ab exercises x 2x10, 5 d/wk, 6 wk: no change in abdominal fat. McGill big three (curl-up, side bridge, bird dog).
Sources: Vispute 2011 JSCR 25:2559, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0b013e3181fb4a46. McGill 2010 Strength Cond J 32(3):33, DOI 10.1519/SSC.0b013e3181df4521.
Plain: Ab exercises strengthen the core but don't burn belly fat.

## 17. Periodisation / deloads
Rule: Periodised > non-periodised for 1RM, ES 0.43; undulating best. Deload: 1-week midpoint deload -> no hypertrophy difference. Athlete practice: ~6-day deload every ~5.6 weeks.
Sources: Williams 2017 Sports Med 47:2083, DOI 10.1007/s40279-017-0734-y. Coleman 2024 PeerJ 12:e16777, DOI 10.7717/peerj.16777. Rogerson 2024 Sports Med Open 10:26. Bell 2023 Sports Med Open 9:87.
Flag: deload timing evidence is weak. Encode "optional every ~5–6 weeks".
Plain: Vary the load over time; a planned easy week every month or so is common practice but not proven.

## 18. Split by days available
Rule: Split vs full-body: no difference when volume equated (14 studies, n=392). Justification: each muscle >=2x/wk -> 1–2 d: full-body; 3 d: full-body (or U/L/full); 4 d: upper/lower; 5–6 d: push/pull/legs.
Sources: Ramos-Campo 2024 JSCR 38:1330, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000004774. Evangelista 2021 Einstein 19:eAO5781. Bartolomei 2021 JSCR 35:1520, DOI 10.1519/JSC.0000000000003573.
Plain: The split is a scheduling tool; pick the one that lets you train every muscle twice a week with the days you actually have.

Unverifiable/weak, summarised: ACSM "48–72 h" (not in text); AAP 2020 and Lloyd 2014 numbers (paywalled); 85% ramp step (convention); low-impact-first (convention); deload timing (weak); McGill content (light check); Pelland per-session figures (preprint).
