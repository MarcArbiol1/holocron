## Key facts with sources

Copyright (names are not the issue).
- US Copyright Office Circular 33: "Names, titles, slogans, and short phrases are not protected by copyright." https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf
- EU: short phrases, names and titles are free for anyone to use unless they meet the Infopaq originality test. https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/public-domain-2020-11-19_en ; https://www.zacco.com/en/insights/articles/protection-of-titles-under-copyright-law-and-trademark-law-knud-wallberg/
- Spain LPI art. 10.2 protects a title only "cuando sea original". https://www.iberley.es/legislacion/articulo-10-ley-propiedad-intelectual

Trademark law hinges on commerce. US: "use in commerce ... likely to cause confusion" (https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/1114); EU: use "in the course of trade" (https://ipright.eu/trademark-regulation/en/Article-9).

Registrations verified on USPTO TSDR / TTABVUE.
- PALANTIR, Palantir Technologies, Reg. 3671386, class 42, live: https://tsdr.uspto.gov/statusview/sn77111698 ; UK00911251485: https://www.trademarkelite.com/uk/trademark/trademark-detail/UK00911251485/PALANTIR
- Palantir opposed EnChannel Medical's PALANTIR; sustained 2 Feb 2026: https://ttabvue.uspto.gov/ttabvue/v?pno=91301938&pty=OPP . Counter-point: Palantir.net (web design, 1996) has coexisted 20+ years: https://uspto.report/TM/76399465

Both used characters, art and world content, not just a word. No documented case found of any of these owners acting against a free open-source project over a borrowed name alone.

Platform policies.
- GitHub DMCA is copyright-only: https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/content-removal-policies/dmca-takedown-policy . Trademark policy: "using another's trademark in a way that has nothing to do with the product or service for which the trademark was granted is not a trademark policy violation": https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/content-removal-policies/github-trademark-policy
- Apple 5.2.1 / 4.1(c) / 2.3.7: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ ; rejections: https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/84709 ; https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/791158
- Google Play: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9888072?hl=en

Mitigations. Disclaimers and free distribution "offer no protection from a claim of infringement" (https://grr.com/publications/spoiler-alert-legal-risks-works-created-fans/), though a prominent "not official" notice can help and nominative use is allowed (https://www.dmlp.org/legal-guide/using-trademarks-others). Search registers before naming (https://corsearch.com/content-library/blog/a-practical-guide-to-protecting-your-indie-game/); rebrand rather than fight (https://odinlaw.com/blog-fan-games-legal-risks/).

Not verified: EUIPO/TMview could not be queried directly; Holocron's absence from registers is based on aggregator searches; the 2020 MORDOR clothing filing's status; whether the PADAWAN cancellation was on the merits or by default.

## Bottom line

- Nobody can copyright a word. The only live question is trademark, and trademark only bites when you are selling or promoting something in the market.
- A free GitHub repo is close to zero risk for every name on the list. Worst realistic case is a polite email asking you to rename.
- The moment it goes on the App Store, the app's own name matters most. "Holocron" is the one word to change before submitting.
- Page and level names (Mordor, The Palantir, Padawan) are low risk, but keep them in one config file so they can be swapped in five minutes.
- Never add the extra that turns a word into a case: no Star Wars/LOTR fonts, logos, silhouettes, sound bites, or "Star Wars fitness" in marketing.
